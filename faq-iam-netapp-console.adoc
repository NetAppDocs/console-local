= Identity and access management FAQ for NetApp Console
:hardbreaks:
:nofooter:
:icons: font
:linkattrs:
:imagesdir: ./media/

[.lead]
This FAQ answers common questions about identity and access management (IAM) in the NetApp Console. It focuses on role-based access control concepts, hierarchy behavior, and member management that are useful when planning and implementing access for your organization.

== Getting started

.What are the first steps to set up IAM after creating a new NetApp Console organization?

When you sign up, the Console creates an organization with one Organization admin and one default fleet. From there, the recommended setup steps are:

. Edit the default fleet or create additional fleets and folders to match your business hierarchy.
. Add members to your organization.
. Add or discover resources.
. Associate resources with additional fleets as needed.

== Resource hierarchy: organizations, folders, and fleets

.What is the difference between a folder and a fleet in NetApp Console IAM?

Folders group related fleets for organizational structure and role delegation. fleets contain resources and are where members access those resources. Resources cannot be directly associated with folders — they must be associated with fleets. Assigning a role at the folder level gives that member inherited access to all child fleets and folders.

.Are folders visible to all members of my organization?

No. Folders are only visible to members who have IAM permissions: Organization admin, Folder or fleet admin, or Super admin. Regular members access fleets directly and do not see folders.

.Can I associate a resource directly with a folder?

No. Resources must be associated with fleets, not folders. However, an Organization admin can associate a resource with a folder so that a Folder or fleet admin can then link it to the appropriate fleets within that folder.

.How deep can I nest folders and fleets in my organization's hierarchy?

You can create up to seven levels of folders and fleets in your organization's resource structure.

.What types of resources can be associated with fleets in NetApp Console?

Resources that can be associated with fleets include:

* Storage systems
* Some Backup and Recovery workloads


== Roles and permissions

.What is the difference between an Organization admin and a Folder or fleet admin?

An Organization admin has unrestricted access to all fleets and folders across the entire organization. A Folder or fleet admin can only manage the specific folders and fleets they are assigned. Folder or fleet admins can add members and manage resources within their assigned folders or fleets, but they cannot access or manage resources outside of their assigned areas.


.What role should I assign to a user who only needs to monitor storage health and manage support cases?

Assign the *Operation support analyst* role, which provides access to alerts and monitoring tools and the ability to enter and manage support cases.

.Can I change a member's role at the fleet level if that access was inherited from a folder?

No. You cannot change member access at a lower level if it was inherited from a higher level. To modify the access, you must change the member's permission at the higher hierarchy level (folder or organization) where it was originally assigned.

== Managing members and access

.What must happen before I can add a user to my NetApp Console organization?

The user must have already signed up for the NetApp Console before you can add them to your organization, folder, or fleet. This requirement applies even to users who are members of a federated group.

.Do federated users automatically get access to resources when I add their group to the Console?

No. Even when a federated group is assigned a role, individual users must meet two requirements before they can access resources:

. The user must have already signed up for the NetApp Console.
. The user must be explicitly assigned a role in the Console.

NetApp recommends assigning a minimum access role such as Organization viewer to these users.

== Ongoing hierarchy maintenance

.What happens to access when I delete a fleet?

Members lose access to resources that were available only through that fleet. Before deleting a fleet, move resources to another fleet and update member assignments so the right teams retain access.

.How do I move a resource from one fleet to another?

Remove the resource association from the current fleet and then associate the resource with the target fleet. Complete both actions in one maintenance window to minimize access disruption.

.How do I update access after a team or business change?

Use a fleet-centric approach when you need to update everyone assigned to one fleet. Use a member-centric approach when you need to update one member across many fleets or folders.
