= Manage member access in NetApp Console
:hardbreaks:
:nofooter:
:icons: font
:linkattrs:
:imagesdir: ./media/

[.lead]
Use this member-centric workflow to manage one member's access across your organization. Assign roles to set permissions and remove members when they leave.

If you want to manage access from a specific fleet or folder, use the fleet-centric workflow instead. link:task-users-fleets-assign.html[Manage fleet access assignments].

.Required access roles
Super admin, Org admin, or Folder or fleet admin (for folders and fleets that they are administering). link:reference-iam-predefined-roles.html[Learn about access roles].

You can assign access roles on a fleet or folder basis. For example, assign a role to a member for two specific fleets or assign the role at the folder level to give a member the Ransomware Resilience admin role for all fleets in a folder.

NOTE: Add your folders and fleets before assigning member access. link:task-iam-manage-folders-fleets.html[Learn how to add folders and fleets.]

== Understand how access is granted in NetApp Console           
NetApp Console uses a role-based access control (RBAC) model to manage member permissions. You can assign predefined roles to members individually or through federated groups. You can add and assign roles to service accounts, as well as federated groups. Each role defines what actions a member can perform at the associated resources.

Note the following about granting access in NetApp Console:

* All users must first sign up for the NetApp Console before they can be granted access to resources.
* You must explicitly assign a role to each user in the Console before they can access resources, even if they are members of a federated group that has been assigned a role.   
* You can add service accounts directly from the Console and assign them roles.



[#role-inheritance]
=== Using role inheritance
When you assign a role at the organization, folder, or fleet level in NetApp Console, that role is automatically inherited by all resources within the selected scope. For example, folder-level roles apply to all contained fleets, while fleet-level roles apply to all resources within that fleet.

To understand which resources and permissions are available to a member, view the roles assigned at different levels of your organization's resource hierarchy.

== Assign or modify member access

After a member signs up for NetApp Console, you can add them to your organization and assign them a role to provide access to resources. link:task-iam-manage-members-permissions.html#add-members[Learn how to add members to your organization.^]

You can adjust a member's access by adding or removing roles as needed.


==  Add an access role to a member

You typically assign a role when adding a member to your organization, but you can update it at any time by removing or adding roles.

You can assign a member an access role for your organization, folder, or fleet.

Members can have multiple roles within the same fleet and in different fleets. For example, smaller organizations may assign all available access roles to the same user, while larger organizations may have users do more specialized tasks. Alternatively, you could also assign one user the Ransomware Resilience admin role at the organization level. In that example, the user would be able to perform Ransomware Resilience tasks on all fleets within your organization. 

Your access role strategy should align with the way you have organized your NetApp resources. 


.Steps

. Select *Administration > Identity and access*.

. Select *Members*.

. Select one of the member tabs: *Users*, *Service accounts*, or *Federated groups*.

== Change a member's assigned role

NOTE: Users must have at least one role assigned to them. You can't remove all roles from a user. If you need to remove all roles, you must delete the user from your organization.

.Steps

. Select *Administration > Identity and access*.

. Select *Members*.

. Select one of the member tabs: *Users*, *Service accounts*, or *Federated groups*.

== Remove a member from your organization

Remove a member if they leave your organization.

When you remove a member, the system revokes their Console permissions but retains their Console and NetApp Support Site accounts.

[NOTE] 
====
.Federated members
* Federated users automatically lose access to the NetApp Console when they are removed from your IdP. But you should still remove them from your Console organization to keep your member list up to date.
* If you remove a user from a federated group in your IdP, they lose the Console access associated with that group. However, they still retain any access associated with an explicit role assigned to them in the Console.

====

.Steps

. Select *Administration > Identity and access*.

. Select *Members*.

. Select one of the member tabs: *Users*, *Service accounts*, or *Federated groups*.

. From the *Members* page, navigate to a member in the table, select image:icon-action.png["An icon that is three side-by-side dots"] then select *Delete user*.

. Confirm that you want to remove the member from your organization.
