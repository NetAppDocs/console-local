= Investigate high throughput demand on a volume in NetApp Console Local
:hardbreaks:
:nofooter:
:icons: font
:linkattrs:
:imagesdir: ./media/

[.lead]
When a volume's workload is consuming more IOPS or throughput than expected, use the Workload Analyzer to examine demand patterns over time. The throughput analysis section shows IOPS and MB/s with an optional read, write, and other breakdown so you can identify what type of I/O is driving high demand and whether that demand is trending toward a QoS limit.

.Steps

. In the NetApp Console Local, navigate to *Workload Analyzer*.
+
Alternatively, from the storage inventory, select a volume and click *Analyze*.

. In the *Volume* field, search for and select the volume you want to investigate.

. Set the *Time Range* to cover the period of high demand, then click *Analyze*.

. Scroll to the *Throughput Analysis* section.

. Open the *View* dropdown and select *Breakdown (RWO)*.
+
The charts split into read, write, and other components for both IOPS and MB/s. This lets you determine whether demand is driven by read-heavy access patterns, write-heavy patterns, or a combination.

. Use the component picker to enable or disable the metrics you want to examine:
+
* *Read IOPS* and *Write IOPS* — operations per second by I/O direction
* *Read MB/s* and *Write MB/s* — throughput in megabytes per second by I/O direction
* *Other IOPS* and *Other MB/s* — metadata and other non-data operations
* *QoS IOPS Limit* and *QoS MB/s Limit* — policy ceilings, shown only when a QoS policy is attached to the volume

. Hover over a peak in the chart to see the exact values and percentage breakdown for each component at that point in time.
+
The hover tooltip also shows the average I/O size (throughput divided by IOPS) for each category, which can indicate whether the workload is performing large sequential I/O or small random I/O.

. Enable *Show Forecast* to project whether current throughput trends will reach the QoS IOPS or MB/s limit.
+
If the forecast line is approaching a QoS limit line, the workload may be throttled in the near future.

. If you want to see aggregate demand without the breakdown, open the *View* dropdown and select *Totals*.
+
The totals view shows a single IOPS line and a single MB/s line, which is useful for a quick overall demand summary.

.After you finish

Use the throughput patterns you observe to guide your next steps:

* If read IOPS are very high relative to write IOPS, consider whether read-ahead settings or caching can reduce the load on the volume.
* If the workload is approaching or has reached the QoS IOPS or MB/s limit, see link:task-analyzer-volume-qos-throttling-identify.html[Identify QoS policy throttling on a volume] to confirm throttling and determine whether the limit needs adjustment.
* If throughput is high and latency is also elevated, check the *Resource Utilization* section to determine whether the underlying node or aggregate is under contention. See link:task-analyzer-volume-resource-contention-identify.html[Identify resource contention affecting a volume].
